Hospital Database and Analytics: PostgreSQL to Tableau

Database design · SQL · Tableau · Course project

Phase 1 (schema design and build) is my own work. For Phase 2, the course provided a reference schema and a data generator, and I loaded the data and built the dashboard and analysis.

8 tables Normalized schema with an insurance table and a diagnosis link table
6 charts, 4 KPIs One Tableau dashboard on appointments, revenue, and no-shows
Synthetic data A fictional hospital; no real patients

The problem

Sunrise General Hospital is a fictional hospital from a course scenario. It needs trustworthy data to optimize staffing and reduce cost. The task was to turn a business narrative into a database, then use the data to show where appointments and revenue come from and where time is lost.

What I did

  • Designed the schema: Turned the narrative into an ERD with cardinalities and modality, then into a normalized PostgreSQL schema of 8 tables.
  • Enforced data quality in the database: Wrote constraints for appointment status, patient sex, non-negative charges, and a rule that the amount paid can never exceed the total charge. Each appointment has at most one bill.
  • Tested it: Seeded a small dataset and ran sanity checks: a row count, a doctor-to-appointment join, and a filter for patients without insurance.
  • Loaded the full data: Loaded the course's generated CSV files into PostgreSQL through DBeaver, in foreign key order.
  • Built the dashboard: Connected Tableau to the database, joined the tables, and built six charts and four KPI tiles on one dashboard, with highlight and filter actions.

The database

Rules the database enforces

  • Domain constraint on appointment.status: Scheduled, Completed, No-Show, Cancelled CHECK
  • Domain constraint on patients.sex: F, M, O CHECK
  • Non-negative total_charge and amt_paid CHECK
  • Cross-column rule: amt_paid <= total_charge CHECK
  • billing_record.apt_id is a unique foreign key, so each appointment has 0..1 bills UNIQUE FK
  • patients.insurance_id is a nullable foreign key (optional participation) Nullable FK
  • diagnoses_apt resolves the many-to-many between appointments and diagnoses Junction table

The dashboard

My Tableau dashboard, built on a course-provided dataset. Click to view full size.

What the dashboard shows

  • Orthopedics brings in the most, with an amount paid roughly ten times Oncology's. It also has the most appointments, and Neurology and Oncology have the fewest.
  • Orthopedics has the shortest visits (about 32 minutes) while Neurology and Oncology run longest (about 45 minutes), so the department with the shortest visits earns the most.
  • The most common diagnoses are asthma, fracture, and migraine.
  • At least one appointment is a no-show every month, and some months have three.
  • Doctor workload is uneven: from 1 to 10 appointments per doctor.

What I'd recommend

  • Add staff or extend hours in the busiest departments, and space appointments better.
  • Cut no-shows with reminders by text or email, confirmation before the visit, and easy rescheduling.
  • Balance doctor workload and review whether Oncology's staffing matches its demand.

Limits

  • Two rules from the scenario are not enforced by constraints in my schema: that only completed appointments store a duration, and that billing records exist only for completed appointments. My seed data follows them, but the database would not stop a bad row.
  • The full dataset and the Phase 2 schema came from the course, so the findings describe a simulated hospital.
  • The no-show chart shows counts per month, not a rate.
