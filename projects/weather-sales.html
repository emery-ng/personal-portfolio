Weather-Sales Prediction

Demand forecasting · Client work · Coastal Maine restaurant

Client name withheld for confidentiality.

2 of 4 locations Outdoor sites analyzed
Delivered to client Evaluating rollout across all 4 locations
Key finding Rain and humidity, not heat or cold, predicted lower sales

The problem

A coastal Maine restaurant with four locations was planning inventory for its bestselling item, lobster rolls, on intuition and past experience. Over-ordering led to excess inventory and food waste, and under-ordering led to stockouts during peak periods. Management suspected weather was affecting demand but had no way to measure it, so I analyzed the two outdoor locations.

What I did

  • Built the dataset: Merged Square POS sales data (2022–2026) with Open-Meteo weather data, grouped each day into three service windows (9AM–2PM, 2PM–5PM, and 5PM–close), and analyzed the restaurant's lobster purchasing records, comparing what was ordered with what was left over, to see how often they over- or under-ordered.
  • Fit the model in R: Used negative binomial regression, which suits sales counts that vary more than a standard model allows. Predictors were day of week, service window, temperature, humidity, and rainfall. I also built a second model with interaction terms to cross-check the simpler one.
  • Validated it: Tested predictions against held-out 2026 data the model hadn't seen.
  • Used AI, then verified it: Claude accelerated feature testing and code, and I checked the key findings against the raw data before presenting anything to the client.
  1. 01 Collect Sales, weather, purchasing records
  2. 02 Prepare Merged by day, service window
  3. 03 Model and check Negative binomial, held-out data
  4. 04 Deliver Excel forecast tool, inventory planning

What I found

Temperature had little effect. Rain and humidity predicted lower sales, and the pattern held at one location but not the other. I converted the simpler model into an Excel forecast tool that pulls the weather forecast, giving the team a way to plan inventory ahead.

  • Rain Lower sales
  • Humidity Lower sales
  • Temperature Little effect
Summary of the model's findings, no client data. The pattern held at one of the two locations analyzed.

It wasn't bad weather in general. It was rain and humidity specifically.

What it shows

Identifying what drives demand in data a restaurant already had, and building a forecast to support inventory planning. The client is now evaluating a rollout across all four locations.
