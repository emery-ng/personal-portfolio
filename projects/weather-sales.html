Weather-Sales Prediction

Predictive analytics · Client work · Coastal Maine restaurant

Client name withheld for confidentiality.

4 locations Multi-site restaurant business
Client-adopted Model integrated into their ordering system
Rain + humidity Not temperature — the real drivers of demand

The problem

A four-location seafood restaurant, with two outdoor summer locations, was ordering its bestselling item — lobster rolls — on gut instinct. On days they misjudged, unsold inventory went to waste; the team suspected weather played a role but had no way to test it or plan around it.

What I did

Pulled sales data from the restaurant's Square POS system, merged it with external weather data, and cleaned and aligned both by date. Built a regression model in R testing sales against day of week, time, temperature, humidity, and rainfall — using Claude to speed up feature testing, then independently verifying the rain/humidity finding against the raw data before presenting it to the client.

What I found

The pattern nobody expected: temperature barely moved the needle. Rain and humidity were the real predictors of a slow day. That insight reframed how the client thought about their own business — it wasn't "bad weather" broadly, it was specific, measurable conditions.

It wasn't "bad weather" that hurt sales — it was humidity and rain specifically, and temperature not at all.

What it demonstrates

Finding the signal a business had missed in data it already owned, checking it against the raw numbers rather than taking the model's word for it, and being trusted enough to have it built into how the business actually runs.
