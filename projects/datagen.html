Synthetic Data Generator

Built for LooplyAI.

Internal tool Built for scenario testing and prospect demos

The problem

Looply is an AI tool that helps small businesses understand their customers and run marketing campaigns. To test how it handles different business scenarios, and to demo campaigns to prospective customers, the team needs realistic business data. Real customer data can't be shown, and randomly generated data doesn't behave like a real business: orders with no customer, totals that don't add up, dates out of order.

What I built

A generator that produces three linked tables (customers, transactions, and products) for a fictional business, following business rules so the records behave like a real business's. Each business is set up through configuration: its size, menu, prices, and customer mix. I ran it to generate complete datasets for two fictional businesses, then audited the output.

How I built it

  • Designed for the demo: Leadership wanted to demo a campaign to win back customers who had stopped visiting, which needs customers whose visits fade over time. I designed customer types (regulars, occasional visitors, lapsing customers, one-time buyers) with their own visit patterns. The generator currently produces repeat and one-time customers, and adding the lapsing pattern is planned.
  • Familiar, but not real: Advisors and prospects need to recognize a business without mistaking it for anyone's real records, so I designed fictional businesses with plausible menus and prices.
  • Built with AI: Used Claude Code in VS Code to write the generator from those rules, then tested the output instead of taking it at face value.
  • Validated it: Wrote a separate audit script with 82 rule checks across two generated businesses, covering order math, payment totals, links between tables, and customer geography.

What the audit found

  • Order math Passed
  • Payments Passed
  • Links between tables Passed
  • Customer geography Fixed after audit
  • Repeat customers Warning added
Results from the first run of the audit script on two generated businesses. No real customer data.

77 of 82 checks passed on the first run. The five that failed traced back to three causes:

  • Missing states: five states weren't in the phone and ZIP lookups, so those customers got toll-free numbers and New York ZIP codes. Fixed.
  • Overlapping ranges: Massachusetts ZIP codes included Rhode Island's range. Fixed.
  • Impossible setting: the config asked for 100% repeat customers, but 1,000 orders across 600 customers allows at most 400. The generator quietly lowered the target, and now shows a warning.

After the fixes, I re-ran the checks on a fresh sample and the geography rules passed.

The audit tests the output against the generator's own rules. It doesn't prove the data matches any real business.

Business use

Built for Looply to test how its AI performs across business scenarios and to create campaign demos for prospective customers. Because the data is synthetic, no real customer data is ever exposed. Building the data behind Looply’s demos also gave me a close view of how an early-stage company approaches launches, marketing, and decisions.
